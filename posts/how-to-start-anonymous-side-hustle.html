How to Start an Anonymous Side Hustle

Setup, launch, and make your first sale without building a public brand.

Launching a side hustle anonymously is about reducing exposure while maximizing effectiveness. For many people, privacy is a priority: you might want income without public networking, or you may prefer to test ideas quietly. This guide gives a practical, step-by-step plan you can follow and finish in 30 days.

Phase 0: Decide and limit scope (Day 0–1)

Pick a single, narrow deliverable. Narrowness is essential. Good examples: a 7-page guide that teaches one job application trick, a bundle of three tailored cover letters, or a micro-tool that extracts keywords from job descriptions. Avoid broad, long-term products at first.

Phase 1: Lightweight setup (Day 1–3)

  1. Create an anonymous identity: new email (Gmail), brand name (Inkvale style), and a minimal GitHub account if you plan to host tools or pages. Keep the identity consistent across product and Gumroad accounts.
  2. Open a Gumroad account: use your anonymous brand email. Gumroad is ideal because it handles payment, delivery, and VAT.
  3. Prepare a one-page sales page: host on GitHub Pages. A single HTML file with headline, 3 bullets, one screenshot and a Gumroad buy button is enough.

Phase 2: Build the product (Day 4–12)

Draft the product quickly. If it's a PDF guide, use a simple, readable font and include 1–2 screenshots and a short template. If it's a tool, build a minimal single-page web utility. The aim is usefulness over polish. Finish the first draft and export as PDF (for guides) or live URL (for tools).

Phase 3: Launch & test (Day 13–20)

Soft launch to one or two targeted places. Examples: a specific subreddit related to your niche, a targeted Slack group, or a small mailing list. Keep the message helpful, not promotional—explain the problem you solved and offer the product as a practical solution. Offer a small discount for the first 5 buyers (use Gumroad coupons).

Collect feedback via a one-question follow-up email (Gumroad provides buyer emails). Ask: “What did you find most useful, and what should we improve?” Use feedback to sharpen product copy, add an FAQ, and fix any delivery friction.

Phase 4: Track & iterate (Day 21–30)

Install GA4 on all pages (homepage, tool pages, product pages) and track three events:

  • tool_use — when a user runs a tool (we add this in tool templates)
  • begin_checkout — when the product buy button is clicked
  • purchase — on Gumroad redirect / thank-you page (if you enable redirect)

Mark `begin_checkout` and `purchase` as conversions in GA4 so you can see conversion rates. If your conversion from product page to buy is below 1%, improve copy, screenshots, or price. If conversion is above 2.5–3%, consider scaling using small promoted posts or niche newsletters.

Operational tips for anonymity and reliability

  • Use a fresh brand email and do not link it to your primary social profiles.
  • Use Gumroad for checkout; enable immediate file delivery and set a clear refund policy (7 days is standard).
  • Keep the product small and iteratively improve. Each minor update (add a sample, improve a template) can massively increase perceived value.

Finally, treat this like a productization practice: each small product teaches you one market lesson. Repeat the process — the second and third products get easier and your funnels get better.
